Recent peer-reviewed systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and empirical studies examining the impact of Generative AI (GenAI) tools on mathematics achievement among middle school students reveal the following:

1. **Meta-Analyses & Systematic Reviews: Key Findings**

   - **The Effectiveness of AI on K-12 Students' Mathematics Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis**  
     - "Overall, AI interventions—including GenAI tutors, chatbots, automated problem generators—yielded a moderate positive effect size on mathematics achievement compared to traditional instruction."  
     - "Effect Size: SMD ≈ 0.32 (95% CI varies across studies, some subgroup analyses)."  
     - "Outcomes Measured: Standardized test scores, classroom assessments, and conceptual understanding."  
     - "Moderators: Type of AI tool (dialogue-based tutors and adaptive GenAI tools outperformed basic automation), duration (interventions > 8 weeks had stronger results), demographic variables (effects present for both high- and low-SES learners but often larger for students with prior low achievement)."  
     - "Link: http://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s10763-024-10499-7" [9]

   - **Analysing the Impact of Artificial Intelligence and Computational Sciences on Student Performance: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis**  
     - "AI-based math tutoring systems, including conversational agents (chatbots) and adaptive/problem-generation platforms, resulted in positive, significant gains on standardized achievement tests and conceptual math tasks."  
     - "Effect Size: Varied by intervention, typically moderate (g ≈ 0.30-0.40)."  
     - "Variables: Noted that teacher facilitation and blended learning models improved GenAI tool effectiveness; more pronounced impacts in settings with low baseline achievement."  
     - "Link: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1375397" [7]

   - **Meta-Analysis of Artificial Intelligence in Education**  
     - "Generative AI chatbots and automated tutors outperformed 'rules-based' automation and static e-tutors in facilitating math learning gains."  
     - "Effect: Stronger for conceptual understanding (e.g., multi-step reasoning), moderate for computation skills."  
     - "Link: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1465704" [5]

   - **Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Educational Functioning: A Review and Meta-Analysis**  
     - "Large overall positive effects for AI (including GenAI) on cognitive and knowledge utilization outcomes in mathematics."  
     - "Substantial heterogeneity; best results with longer intervention and where teacher guidance supported AI learning."

- "While meta-analytic and review evidence supports small but positive effects of GenAI tools overall, *rigorous, large-scale RCTs focused on high school mathematics achievement are currently lacking* (as of early 2026). Most evidence comes from quasi-experimental or mixed-methods studies, with descriptive and preliminary achievement gains—but with causality unproven at scale."
- Tools examined include "chatbots (for both problem generation and feedback), fully automated math tutors, and classroom support platforms."
- Duration: Most interventions last from "one unit up to a full semester."
- Participant demographics: Variable; "demographic data and subgroup effect reporting are rare."
- Instructional context: "Most studies combine AI tools with conventional math instruction; stand-alone GenAI learning is rare and typically limited to extra support/homework."
- Achievement outcomes: "Improvements are more consistent for conceptual understanding and student confidence/self-efficacy; standardized test or overall grade effects tend to be small but positive."
