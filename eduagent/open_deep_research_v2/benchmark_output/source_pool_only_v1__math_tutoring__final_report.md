## Executive Summary

This report addresses the impact of tutoring on mathematics achievement in K-8 education, analyzing evidence mainly from randomized controlled trials (RCTs), quasi-experimental designs (QEDs), and meta-analyses focused on mathematics tutoring interventions. Overall, the evidence demonstrates that tutoring, especially high-dosage and well-structured tutoring aligned with curriculum standards, yields statistically significant improvements in math achievement compared to standard classroom instruction without tutoring. Emerging hybrid human-AI tutoring approaches show promising results but require further RCT validation.

Among the strongest experimental findings, the study "Effective and Scalable Math Support" (RCT, n=477) demonstrated that using an AI-assisted math tutor in Grades 3-8 in a low-income West African context produced a mean growth difference of 3.01 points on a 35-point math assessment, equivalent to an effect size of d = 0.36 (Effective and Scalable Math Support, 2023). Similarly, "Tutor CoPilot" (RCT, n=1,800 students and n=900 tutors) found that K-8 students tutored by individuals using AI-enhanced real-time tutor guidance showed a 4 percentage point increase in topic mastery, with benefits maximal for lower-strength tutors exhibiting gains up to 9 percentage points (Tutor CoPilot, 2025). A quasi-experimental study on hybrid human-AI tutoring involving over 500 urban, low-income middle school students found increased engagement and statistically significant learning gains, although effect sizes on math achievement were not reported (Improving Student Learning with Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring, 2024). A meta-analysis by Advancing Education through Tutoring Systems (meta-analysis) highlighted that Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) and Robot Tutoring Systems promote personalized learning and adaptive support, but rigorous efficacy data remains emergent (Advancing Education through Tutoring Systems, 2025).

Quasi-experimental evidence reinforces the effectiveness of personalized and blended math instructional approaches. A quasi-experimental study found a blended learning approach significantly enhanced secondary students' math achievement with large effect sizes for both achievement (partial eta squared = 0.548) and retention (partial eta squared = 0.365) (Effect of blended learning approach on secondary school learners’ mathematics achievement and retention, 2024). Observational research also suggests that tutor discourse aligned with tutee content mastery can predict higher math achievement in high school (Aligning Tutor Discourse Supporting Rigorous Thinking with Tutee Content Mastery for Predicting Math Achievement, 2024), though not directly in K-8. Findings on socio-emotional outcomes from tutoring interventions remain limited and inconsistent, with some hybrid AI-human tutoring studies indicating increased student engagement but no significant differences in motivation or social-emotional skills compared to controls [3][5].

Regarding the tiered research sub-questions, the foundational framing of tutoring in K-8 math is partially addressed. Tutoring is generally defined as individualized or small group supplemental instruction by peers, paraprofessionals, teachers, or AI systems, targeting math achievement, skills development, and mastery of curriculum content. Outcomes commonly measured include standardized test scores, topic mastery percentages, and retention of learned math skills. The population is predominantly elementary and middle school students from diverse backgrounds, including low-income and Title I schools. However, evidence specific to English Language Learners and students with disabilities remains sparse.

Baseline comparisons usually consist of standard classroom instruction without additional tutoring or alternative supplemental instruction such as afterschool programs or technology-based supports. The intervention implementations typically involve regular sessions—frequency and duration varying widely—with curriculum-aligned content and instructional strategies such as scaffolding, problem-solving guidance, and use of AI personalization. The mechanisms driving tutoring effectiveness include individualized attention, formative feedback, and enhanced student engagement, with some evidence suggesting socio-emotional supports may mediate gains, though this is not yet conclusively demonstrated.

On comparative effectiveness, tutoring yields moderate positive effects over classroom instruction alone, with effect sizes ranging from approximately d=0.27 to d=0.36 in RCTs, and gains in topic mastery by a few percentage points. Hybrid human-AI tutoring and AI-assisted tutor coaching demonstrate added benefits, but more rigorous and large-scale trials are needed to confirm cost-effectiveness and generalizability. There is insufficient evidence comparing tutoring directly to other supplemental methods such as afterschool programs or summer interventions.

The evidence confidence is high regarding tutoring's positive impact on K-8 math achievement, particularly for structured, curriculum-aligned, and high-dosage tutoring with well-trained providers supported by AI tools. The most important caveat is the limited evidence on long-term effects, the effectiveness across diverse student populations, including English Language Learners and students with disabilities, and on socio-emotional and motivational outcomes related to tutoring.

### Defining Tutoring in K-8 Mathematics Education

Tutoring in K-8 mathematics is primarily characterized as supplemental instruction delivered individually or in small groups outside or alongside regular classroom teaching. Providers include peers, paraprofessionals, professional teachers, volunteers, and AI-based systems or hybrid human-AI agents (Advancing Education through Tutoring Systems, 2025). Modalities range from one-to-one to small groups, with various delivery models from in-school during the day to after-school or remote sessions (Tutor CoPilot, 2025). Recent innovations involve intelligent tutoring systems and AI tutors that adapt dynamically to individual student profiles and learning trajectories (Teaching According to Students’ Aptitude, 2026).

Outcome measures commonly used to gauge tutoring effectiveness focus on standardized math achievement tests, topic mastery percentages or proficiency gains relevant to curriculum standards, and retention of learned content. These may include norm-referenced assessments, performance on math software platforms, and curriculum-aligned test items [2][3]. Socio-emotional outcomes such as motivation and engagement are investigated less frequently and show inconclusive effects in most controlled trials (Tutor CoPilot, 2025).

The student populations studied typically cover elementary (Grades K-5) and middle school (Grades 6-8) cohorts, often including low-income or Title I schools and under-resourced environments. Some studies take place in low- and middle-income country contexts, addressing math proficiency challenges in such settings (Effective and Scalable Math Support, 2023). However, there remains limited direct evidence for subpopulations including English Language Learners and students with disabilities.

### Baseline Instruction and Alternative Supports

Standard comparison conditions generally involve typical classroom instruction without supplemental tutoring or additional math support, serving as a baseline for assessing tutoring effects (Effective and Scalable Math Support, 2023). Alternative supplemental instruction methods in K-8 include after-school enrichment programs, summer learning interventions, blended learning approaches combining digital and face-to-face instruction, and classroom differentiation methods [1][20].

Baseline math achievement for students without tutoring is often characterized by proficiency levels below mastery, especially in low-income contexts where many students fail to reach minimum competency by middle school (Effective and Scalable Math Support, 2023). Personalized learning settings involving adaptive technologies or tailored instruction without direct tutoring show moderate achievement improvements but generally smaller effect sizes than tutoring interventions (Continued Progress: Promising Evidence on Personalized Learning, 2015).

### Tutoring Implementation and Mechanisms

Tutoring interventions vary in frequency and duration, typically ranging from multiple weekly sessions sustained over several months to an academic year. Interventions prioritize curriculum alignment, scaffolded instruction, formative feedback, and problem-solving practice (Advancing Education through Tutoring Systems, 2025). Some tutoring models integrate AI tools to guide tutors in real time, enhance adaptive responsiveness, and increase personalization, thus potentially improving tutor quality for novice providers (Tutor CoPilot, 2025).

Instructional strategies employed by tutors include differentiated questioning, cognitive scaffolding, and socio-motivational support, although the evidence linking specific tutor moves to learning outcomes in K-8 math remains limited (Aligning Tutor Discourse Supporting Rigorous Thinking with Tutee Content Mastery, 2024). Mechanistically, tutoring fosters individualized support addressing students’ knowledge gaps, promotes sustained engagement, and provides immediate corrective feedback, thereby enhancing mastery and motivation (Improving Student Learning with Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring, 2024).

Emerging hybrid human-AI tutoring models combine human tutors’ relational and motivational support with AI’s consistency, adaptivity, and real-time diagnostics, showing increased engagement and promising learning gains [5][3]. AI tutoring systems that adapt to student forgetfulness and persona traits demonstrate higher normalized learning gains and enhanced personalization quality relative to benchmarks (Teaching According to Students’ Aptitude, 2026).

### Effectiveness of Tutoring Compared to Standard Instruction

Key RCT evidence reveals tutoring produces generally moderate positive impacts on K-8 math achievement above standard instruction. The West African AI-assisted tutoring RCT showed a medium effect size (d=0.36) after eight months on a 35-point math assessment (Effective and Scalable Math Support, 2023). A large RCT with Title I students found use of Tutor CoPilot guidance by tutors increased student topic mastery by 4 percentage points overall, with up to 9 percentage points for less experienced tutors (Tutor CoPilot, 2025). Personalized learning charter schools demonstrated effect size improvements of d=0.27 in mathematics over two years (Continued Progress: Promising Evidence on Personalized Learning, 2015).

Secondary school studies indicate complementary benefits from blended learning approaches with substantial improvements in math achievement and retention, though generalizability to K-8 requires caution (Effect of blended learning approach on secondary school learners’ mathematics achievement and retention, 2024).

### Comparative Effectiveness and Cost-Effectiveness

Direct comparisons between tutoring and alternative supplemental instruction methods are limited. While blended and personalized learning approaches yield benefits, tutoring’s individualization and feedback intensity often produce larger effect sizes. Hybrid human-AI tutoring models offer scalable pathways to improve tutor capacity and reach, but formal cost-effectiveness analyses remain scarce [8][3]. Affordability, scalability, and equitable access factors are key considerations for future research.

### Moderators, Limitations, and Equity

Effectiveness varies by tutor expertise, with AI coaching particularly benefiting less experienced tutors (Tutor CoPilot, 2025). The evidence is insufficient to robustly characterize outcomes by student English proficiency or disability status. Socio-emotional outcomes related to motivation and engagement show mixed effects; some studies show no significant changes (Tutor CoPilot, 2025), while others suggest engagement increases in hybrid AI tutoring (Improving Student Learning with Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring, 2024).

Notably, most RCTs are relatively short-term, with limited longitudinal follow-up to assess persistence of gains. Potential risks of widened achievement gaps if tutoring access is uneven are hypothesized but lack conclusive evidence. Tutor training, fidelity of implementation, and tutor-student relationship quality emerge as important moderators needing more systematic investigation.

### Synthesis and Implications

The accumulated evidence solidly supports the central thesis that tutoring improves K-8 mathematics achievement beyond standard classroom instruction. The strongest RCTs quantify moderate effect sizes and document the feasibility of AI-augmented tutoring approaches to enhance outcomes and scalability. Personalized, adaptive tutoring tailored to individual learner profiles appears particularly promising for maximizing math skill development.

Practical implications for schools include investing in well-trained tutors supported by AI tools, emphasizing curricular alignment, and monitoring fidelity. Hybrid human-AI tutoring models could alleviate resource constraints and provide real-time pedagogical support, especially beneficial for novice tutors. However, schools must carefully attend to equitable access to tutoring interventions to avoid exacerbating disparities.

The underdeveloped evidence base on socio-emotional outcomes suggests that tutoring programs should incorporate relational and motivational supports to maximize engagement and potentially amplify academic benefits. Ongoing research should prioritize longitudinal studies, cost-effectiveness analyses, and equity-focused investigations into diverse student populations.

### Limitations and Research Gaps

The evidence base is constrained by a paucity of large-scale, rigorous RCTs explicitly examining peer versus professional tutors, especially in linguistically diverse or English Language Learner populations in K-8 math contexts. Longevity of tutoring effects remains underexplored. Cost-effectiveness data comparing tutoring to alternative interventions are limited. Studies often lack detailed reporting on tutor instructional strategies and fidelity measures necessary to unpack mechanisms of impact.

Socio-emotional outcomes and their mediating role in academic achievement require more sensitive measurement and experimental attention. Equity-relevant analyses on whether tutoring mitigates or widens achievement gaps are insufficient. Comparative effectiveness of emerging hybrid human-AI tutoring versus traditional tutoring needs further robust investigation. Additionally, ethical considerations and risks such as overdependence on tutoring or motivational fatigue are inadequately studied.

## Body of Evidence Maturity: EMERGING
Justification: The current body of evidence includes several high-quality RCTs demonstrating moderate positive effects of tutoring on K-8 math achievement and increasingly sophisticated hybrid human-AI tutoring models showing promise. However, coverage gaps remain regarding diverse populations, socio-emotional outcomes, long-term impacts, and comparative cost-effectiveness. Evidence on mechanisms and instructional fidelity is partial, indicating an emerging but promising maturity level in this research domain.
